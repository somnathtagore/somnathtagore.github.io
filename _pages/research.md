## Current Research Areas

Our lab builds computational and experimental frameworks that connect genomic variation to regulatory state, tumor phenotype, and the surrounding tissue ecosystem. Recent work spans functional mutation classes, single-cell and spatial genomics, metastatic organotropism, chromosomal instability, and human-centered agentic AI. See the [publications](/publications/) and [research blog](/blog/) for the underlying studies.

### 1. Human-centered agentic AI and scientific world models

We are developing agentic systems that can plan analyses, use scientific tools, and synthesize evidence while keeping researchers in control of consequential decisions. The organizing principles are scientific agency, traceable evidence, calibrated uncertainty, and governance. This work complements our biological world models: both aim to make complex state transitions inspectable rather than merely predictive. [Read the framework.](/blog/2026/human-centered-agentic-ai-biomedical-research/)

### 2. Uncertainty-aware multimodal integration

Single-cell, spatial, genomic, and clinical measurements are incomplete and affected by coverage, batch, sampling, and modality-specific noise. We treat those limitations as part of the inference problem. Hierarchical, ensemble, and perturbation-based approaches propagate uncertainty into latent clonal and regulatory states, producing outputs that can be calibrated and stress-tested across cohorts, tissues, and platforms.

### 3. Functional variants, regulatory programs, and malignant cell states

Variants are modeled as context-dependent perturbations of a multiscale tumor state rather than as fixed labels. The goal is to learn how mutations and copy-number changes rewire regulatory programs, chromatin, and phenotype in the presence of other genomic events and environmental constraints. Recent studies distinguish hypermorphic, hypomorphic, and neomorphic mutations across cancers and identify developmental and MAPK-responsive states with distinct dependencies in pancreatic cancer. [Read about the mutation framework.](/blog/2026/pan-cancer-mutation-function/) [Read about pancreatic cancer cell states.](/blog/2026/developmental-mapk-pancreatic-cancer-states/)

### 4. Metastatic evolution, organotropism, and the tumor ecosystem

We model metastasis as a sequence of state transitions shaped by chromosomal instability, tumor-cell plasticity, immune organization, and organ-specific niches. This program combines single-cell and spatial data with human tissue models to distinguish shared metastatic programs from adaptations required for colonization of the brain, liver, bone, lung, and other sites. Current studies connect CIN to microenvironmental remodeling, tumor vasculature to T-cell exclusion, and organ-specific tissue niches to colonization behavior. [Explore the recent studies.](/blog/2026/organ-specific-colonization-tissue-model/)
