Antitumor immunity depends not only on whether T cells are present, but also on whether they can reach malignant cells inside the tumor. This preprint examines a vascular mechanism that helps determine that spatial access.

Endothelial cells isolated from tumor cores and margins identified ICAM-1 as a candidate regulator of T-cell localization. As tumors grew, T cells became excluded from the core while endothelial ICAM-1 became enriched at the periphery. Blocking ICAM-1 restored intratumoral T-cell infiltration, increased effector activity, delayed growth of immunogenic tumors, and sensitized an immune-refractory model to anti-PD-1 therapy.

The findings position the endothelial ICAM-1/LFA-1 axis as a spatial checkpoint in the tumor microenvironment. They also show why spatially resolved analysis can reveal mechanisms that are obscured when immune abundance is measured without location.
