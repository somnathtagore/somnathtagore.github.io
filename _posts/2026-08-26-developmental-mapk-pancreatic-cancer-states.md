Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma contains malignant epithelial cells in multiple molecular states. A central question is whether these states reflect a single continuum or distinct regulatory programs with different biological dependencies.

In this study, single-cell master-regulator analysis across multiple cohorts identified three co-existing developmental lineage states: a poorly differentiated state associated with epithelial–mesenchymal-transition regulators and two well-differentiated states associated with gastrointestinal and pancreatic developmental programs. Each lineage also separated into low- and high-MAPK substates, revealing an additional layer of epigenetic organization.

This state map connects tumor-cell identity to histopathology, spatial organization, plasticity, and genetic dependencies. It provides a framework for asking which regulatory programs maintain each malignant state and where state-selective therapeutic vulnerabilities may emerge.

**Journal:** *Nature Genetics* (2026)  
**Article:** [doi:10.1038/s41588-026-02714-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41588-026-02714-8)
