Chromosomal instability is common in esophageal adenocarcinoma and is associated with aggressive disease. Although micronuclei generated by instability can activate cGAS–STING signaling, that activation does not necessarily produce an effective antitumor immune response.

Across experimental models and human tumors, this study links ongoing chromosomal instability to tumor-intrinsic innate immune signaling, chemokine production, and myeloid-cell recruitment. CXCL8 emerged as a prominent conserved output of this program. The analysis combines whole-genome sequencing, single-nucleus RNA sequencing, multiplex immunophenotyping, and in situ measurement of cGAS-positive micronuclei.

The results illustrate how a genome-level process can remodel the surrounding ecosystem. They also motivate models that connect copy-number dynamics and micronuclei to signaling, cellular composition, and therapeutic response rather than treating chromosomal instability as a single static score.

**Journal:** *Science Advances* (2026)  
**Article:** [doi:10.1126/sciadv.aeb1611](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aeb1611)
