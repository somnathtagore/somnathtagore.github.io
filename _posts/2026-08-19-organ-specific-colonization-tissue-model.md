Metastatic cells must survive transit, enter distant tissues, and adapt to microenvironments that differ profoundly across organs. Human experimental systems that preserve these organ-specific interactions are therefore essential for studying colonization directly.

This work established a multitissue model in which engineered bone and lung tissues are linked by vascular flow containing circulating cancer cells. Parental breast-cancer cells colonized both tissues and acquired local transcriptional programs, with particularly strong adaptation after homing to bone.

Bone- and lung-tropic derivatives retained organ-specific behavior in independently perfused platforms. Bone-tropic cells produced stronger bone colonization and osteolysis, whereas lung-tropic cells caused greater disruption of lung epithelium. The system creates a bridge between mechanistic tissue engineering and computational models of metastatic state transitions.

**Journal:** *Science Translational Medicine* (2026)  
**Article:** [doi:10.1126/scitranslmed.adv6871](https://doi.org/10.1126/scitranslmed.adv6871)
